# Golden Retriever Grooming: Shedding Control and Coat Maintenance

**Golden Retrievers have a thick water-resistant double coat that sheds throughout the year and more heavily at some times. Weekly brushing is a practical baseline, with daily care during heavy shedding.**

Routine grooming should preserve the body coat while keeping feathering, paws, ears and hygiene areas manageable.

The breed has:

- a dense undercoat;
- longer protective outer hair;
- feathering on the chest, legs, tail and body;
- and a coat designed to repel water.

This coat needs brushing and undercoat removal rather than routine close shaving.

## A practical schedule

| Task | Starting schedule |
|---|---|
| Brushing | Weekly |
| Heavy shedding | Daily or more frequent |
| Bathing | Often around monthly, adjusted individually |
| Professional coat care | Based on shedding, activity and coat |
| Nails | Regular checks |
| Ears and paws | Check during grooming |

The dog’s activity, swimming and skin condition change the plan.

## Managing shedding

During heavy shedding:

- increase brushing;
- remove loose undercoat;
- check friction areas;
- and consider professional deshedding.

Deshedding reduces loose coat but cannot stop the natural cycle.

Read [Dog Deshedding](https://smspetgrooming.ae/dog-deshedding/).

## Bathing and drying

A Golden may need bathing when dirty, after swimming or within a professional maintenance schedule.

The dense coat must be:

- fully rinsed;
- dried to the skin;
- and checked for tangles.

Persistent skin odour, itching or discharge requires veterinary assessment.

## Feathering and outline trimming

Professional tidying may include:

- paws;
- ears;
- leg feathering;
- tail outline;
- and hygiene areas.

The aim is a neat natural outline, not removal of the complete body coat.

Routine close shaving is not recommended for a healthy double coat.

Possible exceptions include severe matting, medical access or another individual welfare need.

## Ears, nails and paws

Golden Retrievers that swim or have heavy ear feathering need regular observation.

Seek veterinary advice for:

- ear pain;
- discharge;
- strong odour;
- swelling;
- or repeated head shaking.

Nails should be checked according to growth and activity.

## Frequently asked questions

### How often should a Golden Retriever be brushed?

Weekly is a practical baseline, with daily brushing during heavy shedding.

### Should a Golden Retriever be shaved in summer?

Routine shaving is not required and may affect the double coat.

### How often should a Golden Retriever be bathed?

Often around monthly or when dirty, adjusted for swimming, skin and lifestyle.

### Can feathering be trimmed?

Yes. Feet, ears, legs and outline may be tidied without shaving the body.

### Does deshedding stop shedding?

No. It reduces loose dead coat but does not stop growth.
