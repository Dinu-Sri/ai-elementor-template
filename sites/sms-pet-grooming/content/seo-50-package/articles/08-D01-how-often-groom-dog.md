# How Often Should You Groom a Dog? A Schedule by Coat Type and Breed

Many dogs that receive regular professional grooming follow a **four-to-six-week appointment cycle**, but that is not the correct schedule for every coat. Smooth short coats may need professional grooming less often. Long, curly and continuously growing coats usually require more frequent home care and regular haircuts.

The most accurate answer separates grooming into five schedules: brushing, bathing, nail care, deshedding and haircuts.

| Coat type | Home brushing | Professional grooming starting point |
|---|---|---|
| Smooth or short coat | About weekly; more during shedding | Every six to twelve weeks or as needed for bath, nails and hygiene |
| Medium shedding coat | Several times a week | Around six to eight weeks, adjusted for shedding and coat condition |
| Long or silky coat | Daily or near-daily | Often every four to six weeks |
| Curly or continuously growing coat | Daily coat checks and thorough brushing | Often every four to six weeks for clipping |
| Double coat | Several times a week; daily during a heavy shed | Seasonal deshedding or around six to eight weeks according to need |
| Wire coat | Weekly maintenance | About six to twelve weeks depending on trim or hand-stripping style |

These ranges are starting points. Breed, activity, health, coat length and the owner’s home maintenance can change them.

## Grooming is several schedules, not one

### Brushing

Brushing removes loose coat, distributes natural oils and helps prevent tangles. Long, silky and curly coats commonly need daily brushing. Short coats may need less frequent work, but still benefit from regular checks.

### Bathing

Most dogs need bathing when the coat becomes dirty or develops odour. Non-shedding breeds without skin problems are often bathed around every six to eight weeks, while active dogs may need cleaning sooner.

Dogs receiving medicated shampoo should follow a veterinarian’s plan.

### Nail care

Many dogs need nail attention around monthly. Dogs walking frequently on rough pavement may have more natural wear, while inactive dogs and dewclaws can require earlier care.

### Deshedding

Deshedding is useful when loose undercoat builds up. It is especially relevant for double-coated breeds and heavy seasonal shedders.

### Haircuts and coat trimming

Continuously growing coats, including Poodles and Shih Tzus, usually need regular professional clipping. Four to six weeks is a common maintenance interval for a short practical pet trim.

## Smooth and short-coated dogs

Short coats do not need haircuts, but they still need grooming.

A weekly brush can remove loose hair and help the owner inspect the skin. Professional appointments may include bathing, nail care, ears and paws.

A short-haired dog may need grooming sooner when:

- the coat becomes dirty or oily;
- shedding increases;
- the nails are long;
- the dog has rolled in something unpleasant;
- skin or ear changes are noticed; or
- the owner cannot complete routine care safely.

## Long and silky coats

Long coats tangle in friction areas such as behind the ears, under the legs and around the hindquarters. Daily or near-daily brushing is often needed when the coat is kept long.

A four-to-six-week professional schedule helps maintain bathing, drying, outline trimming, nails and hygiene. Waiting longer may be possible when the coat is kept shorter and maintained thoroughly at home.

## Curly and continuously growing coats

Poodles and similar coats can mat close to the skin. The surface may look fluffy while knots develop at the roots.

These dogs usually need:

- frequent thorough brushing;
- regular bathing and complete drying; and
- professional clipping around every four to six weeks.

A longer style requires more home maintenance than a short pet trim.

Double coats have a soft undercoat below protective guard hairs. Routine care focuses on brushing, bathing, complete drying and removing loose undercoat.

During a heavy shed, daily brushing may be helpful. Professional deshedding can remove loose dead coat but cannot stop the normal shedding cycle.

Close shaving is not a standard cooling method for a double coat. Severe matting, medical treatment or individual welfare needs are separate decisions that should be assessed professionally.

## Wire coats

Wire-haired breeds may be maintained through trimming or hand stripping, depending on the desired texture and the owner’s expectations.

The interval can range from approximately six to twelve weeks. A pet trim that uses clippers will produce a different coat texture from a hand-stripped finish.

## Puppy, adult and senior schedules

### Puppies

Start handling and brushing early. The first professional appointment should focus on calm introduction and essential care, with timing based on health, vaccinations, coat need and provider policy.

### Adult dogs

The adult schedule should reflect coat type and lifestyle. A beach-going dog, a dog that spends time outdoors and a mainly indoor companion may need different bathing and coat checks.

### Senior dogs

Older dogs may need more frequent nail checks and adapted appointments. Arthritis, skin changes and reduced mobility can make long sessions difficult.

A sudden coat decline, unusual odour, baldness or increased sensitivity needs veterinary assessment.

## Signs your dog needs grooming sooner

Move the appointment forward when you see:

- tangles or mats;
- nails touching the floor or clicking;
- heavy loose undercoat;
- dirt, oil or odour;
- hair blocking the eyes or hygiene areas;
- debris caught in the coat;
- reduced comfort during movement; or
- difficulty maintaining the coat at home.

Skin redness, wounds, severe itching, sudden hair loss or pain should be discussed with a veterinarian.

## Choosing Basic or Full Grooming

Basic Grooming is normally the starting point for routine bath, coat and hygiene care. Full Grooming is more relevant when a complete haircut or broader trimming is required.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is grooming every four weeks too often for a dog?

Not for a dog with a continuously growing coat or a short maintained haircut. A smooth-coated dog may need professional grooming less often.

### How often should a short-haired dog be groomed?

Brush about weekly and book professional care as needed for bathing, nails, shedding and hygiene. Many short coats can go six to twelve weeks between full appointments.

### How often should a Poodle or Shih Tzu be groomed?

Many are professionally groomed every four to six weeks. Longer styles require more home brushing.

### How often should a double-coated dog be groomed?

Brush several times a week and more during heavy shedding. Professional deshedding may be scheduled seasonally or when loose undercoat builds up.

### Does Dubai weather mean a dog should be shaved?

No. Coat type and condition matter more than temperature alone. Routine shaving is not appropriate for many double coats.
