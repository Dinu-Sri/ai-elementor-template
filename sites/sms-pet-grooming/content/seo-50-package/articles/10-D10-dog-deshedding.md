# Dog Deshedding: How It Works and Which Coats Benefit Most

Dog deshedding removes **loose, dead hair**, especially undercoat that remains trapped below the outer coat. It can reduce the amount of fur released into the home and help prevent compact undercoat from building up.

Deshedding does not stop the normal hair cycle. It is also not the same as shaving, dematting or simply giving the dog a short haircut.

Many dogs have two coat layers:

- an outer coat of guard hairs; and
- a softer undercoat below it.

During shedding, older hairs release from the follicles. Some fall out naturally. Others remain trapped in the coat until brushing, washing, drying and coat-appropriate tools remove them.

Professional deshedding is a planned process that works with the coat rather than cutting it short.

## Which dogs benefit most?

### Double-coated breeds

Dogs such as Huskies, Golden Retrievers, Pomeranians, German Shepherds and many other breeds have a dense undercoat. They can release large amounts of coat during seasonal changes.

### Heavy year-round shedders

Indoor dogs may shed at lower levels throughout the year because the home environment is relatively stable. Regular coat removal can help manage ongoing loose fur.

### Dogs with compact undercoat

A dense coat can become difficult to separate when loose undercoat remains trapped. Professional bathing, drying and brushing can release hair that ordinary surface brushing misses.

### Some short-haired dogs

Short-haired dogs shed too. A coat-appropriate brushing and bathing service can reduce loose hair, but the method is different from treating a thick undercoat.

### Dogs that may not need a deshedding service

Continuously growing coats such as Poodles do not shed in the same way as a double coat. Their main needs are brushing, mat prevention and regular clipping.

A dog with bald patches, inflamed skin, severe itching or sudden hair loss needs veterinary assessment rather than a routine deshedding appointment.

## What happens during a professional deshedding session?

The exact process depends on the coat, but it commonly includes:

### Coat assessment

The groomer checks coat type, density, skin condition, mats, loose undercoat and the dog’s tolerance.

### Brushing before or during preparation

Loose surface hair and tangles are identified. Tight mats are a separate problem and should not be treated as ordinary shedding.

### Bathing with suitable dog products

Bathing helps clean the coat and release dead hair. Medicated products should only be used when appropriate and, where relevant, under veterinary guidance.

### Complete drying

Drying separates the coat and helps move loose undercoat outward. The method must be adjusted for the dog’s comfort, health and behaviour.

### Coat-appropriate removal

Combs, rakes, brushes or deshedding tools are selected for the coat. Veterinary guidance notes that deshedding brushes can remove dead loose undercoat without damaging the topcoat when used appropriately.

### Final coat check

The groomer checks whether loose coat remains, whether the skin looks normal and whether follow-up maintenance is needed.

## Deshedding versus brushing, dematting and shaving

| Service | Main purpose |
|---|---|
| Brushing | Routine removal of loose hair, debris and early tangles |
| Deshedding | Removal of released dead coat and undercoat |
| Dematting | Management of compact tangles attached in the coat |
| Shaving | Cutting the coat very short for style, severe matting, medical access or individual welfare needs |

A dog can need more than one service. For example, a double-coated dog may need deshedding and have one local mat. The groomer should treat each issue separately.

## Does deshedding stop shedding?

No. Normal shedding continues because hair grows, rests and releases as part of a natural cycle.

Deshedding can:

- remove hair that is ready to release;
- reduce loose fur for a period;
- make the coat easier to brush;
- reduce undercoat build-up; and
- help owners manage the home environment.

It cannot permanently prevent hair from shedding.

Be cautious with claims such as “90% shedding reduction.” Results vary by breed, season, coat condition and home care.

There is no universal interval.

A practical schedule may include:

- frequent home brushing during heavy shedding;
- professional deshedding when undercoat becomes difficult to manage;
- seasonal appointments for dogs with pronounced coat changes; or
- a routine interval within the dog’s normal grooming schedule.

The coat should determine the timing. A dog that is no longer releasing much dead coat does not benefit from aggressive repeated tool use.

## Double coats and shaving

Deshedding removes loose undercoat while preserving the outer coat. Shaving cuts the coat.

A close shave is not a routine substitute for deshedding and is not automatically a cooling solution. Guard hairs also help protect the skin from sun and environmental exposure.

Clipping may still be considered for severe matting, medical treatment or an individual welfare reason. Those are separate decisions that should be discussed with a groomer or veterinarian.

## What results should owners expect?

After a suitable deshedding session, owners may notice:

- less loose fur coming out when the dog is touched;
- a coat that feels lighter and more separated;
- easier home brushing; and
- reduced fur accumulation for a period.

The dog will still shed. The result also depends on continuing home maintenance.

## When shedding needs a veterinarian

Normal shedding should not create inflamed skin or sudden bald areas.

Arrange veterinary assessment for:

- bald patches;
- redness, sores or discharge;
- severe or persistent itching;
- a sudden major increase in hair loss;
- broken coat with skin darkening;
- significant dandruff or odour;
- lethargy, weight change or other illness signs; or
- overgrooming or chewing at the coat.

A groomer can report an abnormal appearance but cannot diagnose the cause.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is deshedding good for every dog?

No. It is most useful for dogs releasing loose coat or undercoat. Continuously growing coats have different grooming needs.

### Is deshedding the same as shaving?

No. Deshedding removes dead loose hair. Shaving cuts the coat short.

### How long do deshedding results last?

It varies by coat and season. The dog continues its normal hair cycle, so regular home brushing remains necessary.

### Can deshedding damage the coat?

Aggressive or unsuitable tool use can irritate skin or damage hair. The method should match the coat and stop when loose hair has been removed.

### Why is my dog shedding in patches?

Patchy hair loss is not normal routine shedding. A veterinarian should assess bald areas, skin changes or sudden coat loss.
