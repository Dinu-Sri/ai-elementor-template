# Cat Deshedding: Does It Reduce Loose Fur and Hairballs?

**Cat deshedding removes loose dead hair before it falls around the home or is swallowed during self-grooming. Regular brushing and suitable deshedding may reduce loose fur and hair ingestion, which can help reduce hairballs. It cannot stop the normal hair-growth and shedding cycle.**

Frequent hairballs, bald areas, itching or a sudden change in coat loss should not be treated as an ordinary grooming problem. These signs may need veterinary assessment.

Deshedding is focused removal of loose coat that is ready to come out.

Depending on coat type, the work may use:

- brushing;
- combing;
- coat separation;
- bathing and complete drying;
- or tools designed to lift loose undercoat.

The goal is not to thin healthy attached hair aggressively. Excessive or unsuitable tool use can irritate skin or damage the coat.

## Deshedding versus ordinary brushing

| Service | Main purpose |
|---|---|
| Routine brushing | Remove surface hair, debris and minor tangles while maintaining the coat |
| Deshedding | Remove a larger amount of loose dead coat or undercoat |
| Dematting | Address tangles and mats |
| Shaving | Remove the coat much shorter for style, hygiene, medical access or severe matting |

A short-haired cat may benefit from ordinary brushing without a separate deshedding service. A dense or long-haired cat may release much more loose hair during seasonal or continuous shedding.

## Does deshedding reduce loose fur in the home?

It can reduce the amount of loose hair available to fall onto furniture and clothing.

The effect is temporary because the cat continues to grow and shed hair. The amount of shedding depends on:

- coat type;
- season;
- indoor environment;
- health;
- nutrition;
- age;
- and grooming frequency.

A provider should not promise to stop shedding completely.

## Can deshedding reduce hairballs?

Cats swallow hair while grooming. Removing loose hair before the cat swallows it can reduce the amount entering the digestive system.

This may reduce hairballs, especially in long-haired cats and heavy shedders.

It does not guarantee that hairballs will disappear. A hairball can still occur, and repeated coughing, retching or vomiting should not automatically be assumed to be a harmless hairball.

Cornell Feline Health Center recommends veterinary attention when hairballs occur frequently or are accompanied by changes in appetite, repeated unproductive retching, lethargy, constipation or diarrhoea.

## Which cats may benefit most?

Professional deshedding may be useful for:

- long-haired cats;
- dense-coated cats;
- cats going through heavy seasonal shedding;
- cats that swallow large amounts of loose hair;
- senior or overweight cats that groom less effectively;
- and owners who cannot maintain the coat safely at home.

A short-haired cat with a manageable coat may need only routine brushing.

## Does deshedding help with mats?

Deshedding and dematting are different.

Loose undercoat can contribute to tangles when it remains trapped. Removing loose coat can help prevent future matting.

However, a tight mat is already a separate coat problem. Pulling a deshedding tool through a mat can cause pain.

Severe or skin-tight mats need professional assessment and may require clipping. Never cut close mats with scissors.

There is no fixed interval for every cat.

Consider:

- coat length;
- coat density;
- current shedding;
- brushing routine;
- hairball history;
- skin condition;
- and tolerance of handling.

Some cats may benefit during heavy shedding periods. Others may need routine coat maintenance throughout the year.

The schedule should be adjusted after observing how quickly loose coat returns.

## Bathing and deshedding

Bathing and complete drying can help release loose coat in some cats. This does not mean every cat needs a bath before deshedding.

The provider should consider:

- whether the cat actually needs cleaning;
- whether the coat is tangled;
- whether the cat tolerates water and drying;
- and whether a skin condition is present.

A tangled coat should be assessed before bathing.

## What results should owners expect?

A suitable deshedding appointment may produce:

- less loose hair immediately after grooming;
- a cleaner, separated coat;
- easier home brushing;
- and reduced hair ingestion.

It cannot promise:

- permanent shedding control;
- elimination of every hairball;
- treatment of skin disease;
- or correction of abnormal hair loss.

Realistic expectations protect the cat from unnecessary repeated tool use.

## When shedding needs a veterinarian

Seek veterinary advice for:

- sudden excessive shedding;
- bald patches that do not regrow;
- red or inflamed skin;
- persistent dandruff or greasiness;
- itching;
- wounds;
- excessive licking;
- behaviour change;
- or frequent hairballs or vomiting.

Overgrooming may be linked to skin disease, pain, parasites, stress or another medical issue.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does deshedding stop a cat from shedding?

No. It removes loose hair that is ready to come out but does not stop the normal coat cycle.

### Is deshedding good for short-haired cats?

It can help some dense or heavy-shedding short coats. Other cats need only routine brushing.

### Can deshedding prevent all hairballs?

No. It may reduce swallowed hair and therefore reduce hairballs, but it cannot guarantee prevention.

### Is shaving better than deshedding?

They solve different problems. Shaving changes coat length. Deshedding removes loose coat while preserving attached hair.

### Why is my cat suddenly shedding more?

Season, indoor environment and coat cycle may be involved, but sudden or abnormal coat loss can also indicate illness. A veterinarian should assess concerning changes.
