# Matted Rabbit Fur: Causes, Risks and Professional Grooming Options

**Rabbit mats can pull delicate skin, trap moisture and restrict movement. Mats should never be cut with scissors because rabbit skin is thin and can be drawn into the coat. Severe or felted matting may need an experienced rabbit professional or rabbit-savvy veterinarian.**

The correct response depends on tightness, location, soiling, pain and the rabbit’s ability to tolerate handling.

Common contributors include:

- long coat;
- heavy shedding;
- friction;
- moisture;
- soiling;
- reduced mobility;
- obesity;
- and incomplete daily coat care.

Long-haired rabbits can develop mats very quickly.

## Where mats form

Check:

- under the chin;
- chest;
- neck;
- armpits;
- belly;
- legs;
- feet;
- groin;
- and hindquarters.

Mats near the bottom may be connected to urine or faecal soiling.

## Tangle, mat or felted coat?

| Condition | General meaning |
|---|---|
| Loose tangle | Small area with movement |
| Mat | Compact knot pulling the coat together |
| Skin-tight mat | Little space between coat and skin |
| Felted coat | Broad dense layer of connected matting |
| Soiled mat | Coat mixed with moisture, urine or faeces |

Each level requires a different professional decision.

## Why scissors are dangerous

Rabbit skin is thin and can be pulled upward into a mat.

The person may believe the scissors are under the hair while the skin is also inside the cut.

Do not attempt close mat removal with scissors.

## What professional options may be considered

An experienced professional may discuss:

- gentle separation of a small loose tangle;
- local clipping;
- maintaining a shorter coat;
- broader coat removal;
- or veterinary referral.

The rabbit’s welfare matters more than preserving coat length.

Contact a rabbit-savvy veterinarian for:

- severe felted coat;
- wounds;
- skin infection;
- urine scald;
- persistent dirty bottom;
- pain;
- reduced appetite;
- fewer droppings;
- or a rabbit too distressed for routine handling.

Sedation decisions belong to the veterinarian.

## Long-haired rabbits

Angora, Lionhead and similar coats need daily maintenance.

A professional may recommend a shorter maintained coat when the owner cannot prevent repeated mats.

Read the [long-haired rabbit grooming guide](https://smspetgrooming.ae/long-haired-rabbit-grooming/).

## Preventing repeat matting

Prevention may include:

- daily or frequent brushing;
- early tangle checks;
- a suitable maintained coat length;
- hygiene monitoring;
- mobility assessment;
- and regular professional help.

A monthly appointment cannot replace daily care for a long coat.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can rabbit mats be brushed out?

Small loose tangles may be manageable. Tight or felted mats should not be forced.

### Can I cut a rabbit mat with scissors?

No. The risk of cutting thin skin is high.

### Does a matted rabbit need to be shaved?

The decision depends on severity and health. Broader clipping may be the safest option.

### Will the coat grow back?

It usually regrows, but timing depends on breed, health and coat cycle.

### Can dirty-bottom mats be treated as normal grooming?

Persistent soiling may indicate a medical problem and needs veterinary assessment.
