# Dog Nail Trimming: Frequency, Overgrowth Signs and Professional Help

Most dogs need nail care **about once a month**. Dogs that walk frequently on rough pavement may go longer, while inactive dogs may need a trim about every three weeks.

The calendar is only a starting point. Nails that touch the ground or click clearly on hard floors are already too long. Dewclaws also need separate attention because they do not receive normal ground wear.

| Dog or lifestyle | Practical starting point |
|---|---|
| Average adult dog | About monthly |
| Inactive dog or limited hard-surface walking | Around every three weeks may be needed |
| Dog with frequent pavement walks | May go longer if the nails remain clear of the ground |
| Puppy | Check frequently and introduce calm paw handling early |
| Senior or mobility-limited dog | Check more often because activity and natural wear can decrease |
| Dog with fast-growing nails | Use the individual growth pattern, not the general calendar |

A dog does not need every nail shortened simply because a month has passed. Inspect the paws and use length, movement and comfort as the decision factors.

## Five signs the nails are too long

### 1. The nails touch the ground while standing

When the dog stands naturally on a level surface, the nails should not be supporting body weight.

### 2. You hear clear clicking on hard floors

Consistent clicking is a practical sign that the tips are contacting the floor.

### 3. The dog changes posture or gait

Long nails can change how the paw meets the ground. A dog may shorten the stride, shift weight or become reluctant on smooth floors.

A gait change can also have many medical causes. Do not assume the nails are the only explanation.

### 4. The nails snag or split

Overlong nails are more likely to catch in fabric, carpet or outdoor surfaces.

### 5. The dewclaws curve excessively

Dewclaws sit higher on the leg and often receive no ground wear. They can curve toward the skin if they are not checked.

## Why some dogs need nail care more often

### Activity level

A dog that walks and runs regularly may wear the nails more than a dog with limited mobility.

### Walking surface

Pavement and concrete can file the nails. Grass, carpet and soft sand create less natural wear.

Natural wear is uneven. It should not replace inspection.

### Age and mobility

Senior dogs may move less and need more frequent nail care. Arthritis or pain can also make paw handling difficult.

### Nail shape and growth

Some dogs grow thicker or more curved nails. Previous long-term overgrowth can allow the living quick inside the nail to extend farther, which may require a gradual professional plan.

### Dewclaws

Because dewclaws do not normally contact the ground, they often follow a different schedule from the other nails.

## Puppy and senior-dog considerations

### Puppies

Start with brief positive paw handling. The objective is not to force a complete nail trim. A puppy that learns to accept gentle paw contact may have easier appointments later.

### Senior dogs

Older dogs may need a non-slip surface, supported positioning and a shorter session. Sudden resistance may indicate pain rather than stubborn behaviour.

Tell the groomer about arthritis, spinal problems, weakness, recent injury or medication before the appointment.

A groomer may be appropriate for routine nail care when the dog can be handled safely.

Choose veterinary help when there is:

- a nail growing into the skin or paw pad;
- a broken or bleeding nail;
- swelling or discharge;
- significant pain or limping;
- abnormal nail growth;
- severe fear that may require medication; or
- a medical condition that changes safe handling.

Medication and sedation must be prescribed and managed by a veterinarian.

## What happens during professional nail care?

A professional should first consider:

- how the dog stands and moves;
- the length and shape of each nail;
- dewclaws;
- paw sensitivity;
- the dog’s response to handling; and
- whether routine grooming services are also needed.

The work should stop or be reduced when the dog becomes unsafe or highly distressed. Completing every nail is not more important than the dog’s welfare.

This article intentionally does not provide detailed cutting instructions. Owners who are uncertain about the nail structure or dog handling should use an in-person professional demonstration.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is once a month enough for dog nail trimming?

It is a common starting point. Some dogs need every three weeks, while dogs with substantial natural wear may go longer.

### Are clicking nails too long?

Consistent clicking on a hard floor is a clear sign that the nails are contacting the ground and should be assessed.

### Do pavement walks replace nail trimming?

They can increase natural wear but may not wear every nail evenly. Dewclaws still require checks.

### Why are my dog’s nails still long after a trim?

When nails have been overgrown for a long time, the sensitive quick may extend farther. A gradual professional schedule may be required.

### Can a groomer trim the nails of an anxious dog?

Sometimes. The groomer must assess safety and may reduce or stop the service. Dogs needing medication or sedation require veterinary involvement.
