# African Grey Grooming: Feather, Nail and Beak Care Considerations

**African Greys produce powder down that creates normal fine dander on the feathers and surrounding environment. Grooming should support species-appropriate bathing and preening, assess nails only when overgrown and observe the beak rather than trimming it routinely.**

Sudden feather damage, reduced powder down, abnormal beak growth or illness needs an avian veterinarian.

African Greys are powder-down birds.

Specialised feathers break down into a fine powder that helps condition the plumage. This can appear as light dust on the bird, enclosure and nearby surfaces.

Normal powder should not automatically be treated as dry skin or removed with unverified products.

## Bathing and powder down

Bathing can support feather condition and preening.

African Greys may prefer:

- gentle misting;
- a shower-like environment;
- or another safe method.

Use plain clean water unless an avian veterinarian recommends otherwise.

Do not apply fragrance, oil or skin products to reduce normal powder.

## Nail care and grip

Nails should be assessed by function.

Professional care may be useful when nails:

- interfere with grip;
- change toe position;
- snag repeatedly;
- or grow abnormally.

Sharp nails are not automatically too long.

Read the [bird nail-trimming guide](https://smspetgrooming.ae/bird-nail-trimming/).

## Beak care

A healthy African Grey beak normally grows and wears through eating, climbing and chewing.

Routine trimming is usually unnecessary.

Contact an avian veterinarian for:

- overgrowth;
- cracks;
- bleeding;
- softness;
- asymmetry;
- colour change;
- or eating difficulty.

Read [Bird Beak Care](https://smspetgrooming.ae/bird-beak-care/).

Observe:

- feather symmetry;
- broken feathers;
- bald areas;
- damaged skin;
- excessive picking;
- and changes in powder.

A loss of normal powder, abnormal feather shape or persistent damage may be medically important.

A groomer should not diagnose feather disease.

## Wing trimming and flight

Wing trimming is optional.

African Greys are intelligent active birds that benefit from movement and enrichment. The decision should consider flight skill, household safety, training and welfare.

Read the [wing and feather-trimming guide](https://smspetgrooming.ae/bird-wing-feather-trimming/).

## Handling and stress

Birds have sensitive respiratory systems and can deteriorate quickly under excessive restraint.

A provider should:

- minimise handling time;
- monitor breathing;
- allow recovery;
- and stop when the bird becomes distressed.

Medication or sedation belongs to an avian veterinarian.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is African Grey dander normal?

Yes. Powder down creates normal fine dust.

### How often should an African Grey bathe?

The schedule depends on the bird’s preference, environment and feather condition.

### Does an African Grey need beak trimming?

A healthy beak usually does not need routine trimming.

### Can grooming treat feather plucking?

No. Feather-damaging behaviour requires avian-veterinary assessment.

### Is fragrance safe for an African Grey?

Do not assume it is safe. Avoid unverified products around a bird’s respiratory system.
