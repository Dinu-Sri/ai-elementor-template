# How to Prepare Your Dog for a Mobile Grooming Appointment

**Prepare by confirming the dog’s size, package and desired result; sharing health and behaviour information; providing a normal toilet opportunity; arranging access and parking; and keeping the dog secure for handover. Avoid last-minute forceful dematting or unapproved medication.**

A mobile appointment removes the salon journey, but preparation still matters. The groomer needs accurate information about the dog, coat and location to plan the time, equipment and safest service scope.

### When booking

- Confirm the dog’s approximate weight and size category.
- Choose Basic or Full Grooming.
- Explain the desired coat length or style.
- Report mats, parasites, skin concerns and hygiene problems.
- Disclose medication, pain, mobility issues and previous reactions.
- Confirm the address, parking, gate, lift and access instructions.

### On appointment day

- Give the dog a normal toilet opportunity.
- Keep the routine calm and familiar.
- Keep the dog securely indoors or on a safe lead for handover.
- Separate other pets unless the provider requests otherwise.
- Keep the owner’s phone available.
- Report any new illness, injury or behaviour change.

## Confirm the package and desired result

SMS Pet Grooming offers size-based Basic and Full dog-grooming options at the customer’s Dubai home.

Tell the provider whether the priority is:

- bathing and brushing;
- nail care;
- deshedding;
- hygiene trimming;
- a maintained haircut;
- mat assessment;
- or essential care for a senior or anxious dog.

A reference photo can help, but the finished result depends on coat condition, coat type and the dog’s tolerance. A skin-tight matted coat may not support the style shown in a photo.

Review the current package descriptions on the [mobile dog grooming page](https://smspetgrooming.ae/dog-grooming/).

## Share medical and behaviour information

Report:

- age;
- medication;
- allergies or skin treatment;
- recent surgery;
- arthritis or mobility limitations;
- pain;
- heart or breathing conditions;
- wounds;
- seizures;
- and veterinary restrictions.

Also disclose barking, lunging, snapping, biting, panic, dryer sensitivity or previous incomplete appointments.

This information does not automatically prevent grooming. It allows the provider to decide whether the appointment should be modified, shortened or referred to a veterinarian.

Medication or sedation decisions belong to a veterinarian.

## Should the dog be walked first?

A normal toilet break and suitable light activity may help some dogs settle, but exercise should match the dog’s health, age and weather conditions.

Do not exhaust the dog before grooming. Avoid intense exercise in high heat, especially for flat-faced, senior or medically vulnerable dogs.

The aim is a normal routine, not forced fatigue.

## Should the dog be fed before grooming?

There is no universal fasting rule for routine grooming.

Follow the dog’s normal feeding plan unless the groomer or veterinarian gives specific instructions. Puppies, diabetic dogs and dogs taking medication may need their usual meal timing.

Do not withhold food simply because grooming is booked.

## Prepare the coat without causing pain

Routine brushing is useful when the dog accepts it and the coat is manageable.

Do not:

- pull skin-tight mats;
- use scissors near mats;
- bathe a severely tangled coat before assessment;
- hide the extent of matting;
- or force a long brushing session.

Send clear coat photos when possible. Severe matting may require clipping or shaving rather than brushing.

Read the [dog dematting versus shaving guide](https://smspetgrooming.ae/dog-dematting-vs-shaving/) when the coat is already heavily matted.

Mobile grooming depends on practical access.

Confirm:

- building and villa entry;
- security procedures;
- parking availability;
- gate codes;
- lift access;
- phone contact;
- and the correct location pin.

Tell the provider about height restrictions, paid parking or loading rules before arrival.

Keep the path clear and arrange a safe handover point. Do not allow the dog to rush through an open gate or door.

## Secure other pets and people

Other pets, visitors and excited children can complicate handover.

Where practical:

- keep other animals in a separate area;
- reduce noise;
- limit unnecessary people around the dog;
- and appoint one adult to communicate with the groomer.

The owner should remain reachable throughout the appointment.

## What happens when the groomer arrives?

Repeat the important information at handover:

1. package and desired outcome;
2. medical and medication information;
3. behaviour history;
4. mats or sensitive areas;
5. and the owner’s priorities.

The groomer may adjust the plan after seeing the coat and dog. Essential welfare care should come before an optional cosmetic result.

## After the appointment

Observe the dog’s:

- skin;
- walking;
- comfort;
- drinking;
- appetite;
- and behaviour.

Follow aftercare and brushing advice. Contact a veterinarian for persistent pain, skin injury, breathing changes or a significant health change.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should I bathe my dog before mobile grooming?

Usually no. Bathing is commonly part of the service, and water can tighten some tangles.

### Should I brush out mats before the visit?

Only perform gentle routine brushing. Do not force tight mats or use scissors.

### Can I give calming medication before grooming?

Only when a veterinarian has prescribed it for that dog and appointment.

### Should the owner stay nearby?

Remain reachable and follow the provider’s instructions. Some dogs behave differently when the owner is close.

### What if the dog becomes sick on appointment day?

Contact the provider before arrival. Vomiting, diarrhoea, breathing difficulty, wounds, severe pain or marked tiredness may require postponement and veterinary advice.
