# How to Prepare Your Cat for a Mobile Grooming Appointment

**Prepare by confirming the service and desired result, sharing health and behaviour information, keeping the cat securely indoors and creating a quiet handover area. Avoid last-minute forceful dematting, bathing or unapproved medication.**

A mobile appointment removes the salon journey, but it still involves unfamiliar handling and equipment. Good preparation helps the groomer plan the right scope and reduces preventable delays.

### When booking

- Confirm Basic or Full Grooming.
- Explain the desired result.
- Report mats, skin concerns and hygiene problems.
- Disclose medical conditions and medication.
- Describe previous grooming reactions.
- Confirm the address, building access and parking.
- Ask what the provider needs on appointment day.

### Twenty-four hours before

- Keep the cat’s routine as normal as possible.
- Confirm that the cat is indoors.
- Check doors, windows and balcony access.
- Prepare photos of the coat or desired style.
- Avoid pulling tight mats.
- Do not apply unfamiliar products.

### On appointment day

- Keep the cat in a secure known area.
- Separate other pets.
- Provide a quiet handover route.
- Keep the owner’s phone available.
- Share any last-minute health or behaviour changes.
- Follow the groomer’s instructions about food and owner presence.

## Confirm the correct package and goal

SMS offers Basic and Full cat grooming at the customer’s Dubai home.

Basic is intended for routine coat and hygiene maintenance. Full Grooming is the more relevant starting point when broader trimming or shaving is requested.

Tell the groomer whether the priority is:

- bathing and coat care;
- nail and paw care;
- deshedding;
- hygiene trimming;
- a maintained haircut;
- mat assessment;
- or essential care for a senior or nervous cat.

A style photo is useful, but the coat and the cat’s tolerance determine what can safely be achieved.

## Share health information before the groomer arrives

Report:

- age;
- recent illness;
- wounds;
- skin irritation;
- arthritis or mobility problems;
- breathing or heart conditions;
- medication;
- recent surgery;
- pregnancy;
- parasites;
- and veterinary restrictions.

Also disclose a bite or scratch history. Hiding behaviour information puts the cat and groomer at risk.

A sudden change in coat condition or behaviour may indicate pain or illness. Grooming should not replace veterinary assessment.

## Describe previous grooming behaviour

Tell the provider whether the cat has previously:

- hidden;
- frozen;
- vocalised;
- urinated or defecated;
- struggled intensely;
- bitten or scratched;
- reacted to dryers;
- reacted to clippers;
- or required the service to stop.

A motionless cat is not always calm. Freezing can be a fear response.

This information helps the provider plan a shorter scope, a slower introduction or a veterinary referral.

## Secure the cat before arrival

Keep the cat indoors well before the appointment. Do not wait until the doorbell rings to start searching.

Check:

- external doors;
- windows;
- balcony doors;
- gates;
- and hiding areas that are difficult to access.

Tell family members and building staff that a grooming appointment is scheduled.

A frightened cat can escape through a briefly opened door. Secure handover is part of appointment safety.

## Prepare a quiet environment

Reduce unnecessary noise and activity around the handover.

Where practical:

- move children away from the immediate area;
- separate other animals;
- turn down loud television or music;
- avoid visitors;
- and provide clear access for the groomer.

The provider may use a grooming van or another doorstep setup rather than grooming inside the home. Confirm the exact process during booking.

## Should you brush the cat before the visit?

Light routine brushing is reasonable when the cat enjoys it and the coat is manageable.

Do not:

- pull tight mats;
- cut mats with scissors;
- force a long brushing session;
- bathe a heavily tangled coat;
- or hide the extent of matting.

Water can tighten some tangles. Severe mats may require careful clipping rather than brushing.

Send coat photos when possible so the provider can plan the appointment.

There is no universal fasting rule for routine grooming.

Keep the normal feeding routine unless the provider or veterinarian gives specific instructions. Kittens, cats with medical conditions and cats taking medication may have special needs.

Do not withhold food simply because grooming is scheduled.

If medication or sedation is being considered, that becomes a veterinary matter and may involve different instructions.

## Do not give unapproved calming medication

Never give human medication, someone else’s pet medication or an old prescription before grooming.

Only a veterinarian can decide whether medication or sedation is appropriate.

Owners should also avoid unverified “calming” products unless the veterinarian confirms they are suitable for the individual cat.

## What should be ready for the groomer?

Prepare:

- current contact details;
- the cat’s health and behaviour summary;
- package confirmation;
- coat and style photos;
- building and parking instructions;
- veterinary contact details for a medically complex cat;
- and questions about aftercare.

The provider may ask for a carrier or another secure transfer method. Follow the provider’s instructions.

## What happens when the mobile groomer arrives?

SMS uses a simple service flow:

1. Choose the package.
2. Confirm the time and location.
3. Complete grooming at the doorstep.
4. Receive aftercare and next-visit guidance.

At handover, repeat important information. Do not assume it was remembered from a message sent days earlier.

The groomer may adjust the service after seeing the cat. A reduced scope can be the correct decision when the cat is distressed or the coat is worse than expected.

## What should you do after the appointment?

Allow the cat to return to a quiet familiar area. Observe:

- skin;
- walking;
- appetite;
- grooming;
- litter-box use;
- and behaviour.

Some cats need time to settle. Persistent pain, skin injury, breathing changes or a significant behaviour change requires veterinary advice.

Follow the coat-maintenance schedule given by the groomer so the next appointment does not begin with preventable matting.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should I bathe my cat before the groomer arrives?

Usually no. The bath may be part of the service, and bathing a tangled coat can make mats harder to manage.

### Should I stay during mobile cat grooming?

Provider policies and individual cats differ. Remain reachable and follow the groomer’s instructions about where to wait.

### Can I give my cat a calming tablet before grooming?

Only when a veterinarian has prescribed it for that cat and appointment.

### Should I brush out mats before the appointment?

Do not force painful mats or use scissors. Send photos and allow the groomer to assess them.

### What if my cat hides before the appointment?

Keep the cat in a secure, accessible area before the groomer arrives. Avoid chasing through the home at the last minute.
