# Maine Coon Grooming: Caring for a Large, Long-Haired Cat

**Maine Coons have a thick, shaggy coat that is shorter over the shoulders and longer on the sides, stomach, hind legs and tail. Combing two or three times a week is a practical starting point for many cats.**

The large body and feathered coat make complete coat checks important, even when the breed’s texture is less mat-prone than some long-haired cats.

Key attributes include:

- large body size;
- a shaggy semi-long coat;
- feathering around the legs and tail;
- a neck ruff;
- and longer coat in friction and hygiene areas.

The cat may be difficult for one owner to position safely for a long session.

## A practical grooming schedule

| Task | Starting schedule |
|---|---|
| Full combing | Two or three times weekly |
| Friction-area checks | More frequently |
| Heavy shedding | Increase loose-coat removal |
| Nails | Regular checks |
| Bathing | Occasional and condition-based |
| Professional care | Based on coat, size, mats and tolerance |

Individual coat density varies.

## High-risk areas for tangles

Check:

- behind the ears;
- armpits;
- belly;
- groin;
- hind legs;
- tail base;
- and under the neck ruff.

Separate the coat carefully enough to see the skin without pulling.

Do not use scissors on tight mats.

## Managing shedding and hair ingestion

Regular combing removes loose hair before the cat swallows or sheds as much of it.

Professional deshedding may help when the coat is dense or the owner cannot reach the complete body.

Frequent hairballs, repeated vomiting or sudden coat changes need veterinary assessment.

Most healthy cats do not need frequent baths.

Bathing may help when:

- the coat is dirty;
- oily build-up develops;
- the cat cannot self-groom;
- or a veterinarian recommends a bathing plan.

A dense coat needs complete drying.

## Haircuts and hygiene trims

A local hygiene trim may help when long hair collects debris.

A broader haircut may be discussed for recurring mats or owner maintenance limits.

Routine shaving is not required only because the cat is large or long-haired.

Read [Cat Haircut vs Trim vs Shave](https://smspetgrooming.ae/cat-haircut-vs-trim-vs-shave/).

## Handling a large cat

A Maine Coon appointment needs:

- a stable non-slip surface;
- sufficient space;
- supported positioning;
- short breaks;
- and honest disclosure of behaviour.

A full package should not be promised before the provider understands the cat’s size and tolerance.

## Frequently asked questions

### How often should a Maine Coon be combed?

Two or three times a week is a useful starting point.

### Do Maine Coons mat easily?

They may mat less than some cotton-coated breeds, but friction areas still require frequent checks.

### Should a Maine Coon be shaved?

Not routinely. Severe mats, hygiene or medical need may justify coat removal.

### Do Maine Coons need professional grooming?

Some can be maintained at home. Others benefit from professional coat, nail and hygiene care.

### How long does a Maine Coon appointment take?

It depends on size, coat, behaviour and requested service. The provider should assess the individual cat.
